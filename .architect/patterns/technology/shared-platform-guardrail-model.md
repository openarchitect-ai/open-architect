# Shared Platform Guardrail Model

## Purpose

Define the minimum controls, standards, and paved-road expectations a shared
platform enforces so delivery teams can move quickly within clear boundaries.

## When To Use

- many teams build on a common platform
- governance needs repeatable controls without centralizing every decision
- the platform should enable autonomy while preventing unsafe drift

## When Not To Use

- the environment is too small for a platform model to matter
- the organization is not ready to operate shared guardrails consistently

## Assumptions

- a shared platform exists or is being established
- some controls can be standardized and automated
- teams will adopt paved roads if they are practical and well-supported

## Key Components

- mandatory guardrails
- recommended paved roads
- exception process
- ownership and feedback loop for guardrail evolution

## Benefits

- improves consistency without full central bottlenecking
- supports safer team autonomy
- makes platform expectations more explicit and reusable

## Tradeoffs

- requires investment in enablement and automation
- can create friction if guardrails are too rigid or poorly designed
- needs active maintenance as platform capabilities evolve

## Common Risks

- guardrails defined in theory but not enforced in practice
- teams bypassing the platform because paved roads are weak
- too many guardrails reducing autonomy and delivery speed
