# Transition Hypercare Exit Criteria

## Purpose

Define explicit criteria for leaving hypercare so transition teams know when a
change has stabilized enough to move from elevated support into normal
operations.

## When To Use

- significant releases or migrations include a hypercare period
- handoff from transition support to steady-state operations needs clarity
- stakeholders need objective signals before ending elevated monitoring

## When Not To Use

- the change is too minor to justify a dedicated hypercare phase
- support ownership never changes and no exit decision is needed

## Assumptions

- stabilization indicators can be defined
- operations and delivery teams share the same exit expectations
- evidence can be collected during the hypercare period

## Key Components

- hypercare objectives
- measurable exit criteria
- evidence and review checkpoint
- handoff or closure decision

## Benefits

- prevents arbitrary or politically driven hypercare duration
- improves confidence in transition handoff decisions
- supports clearer accountability between project and operational teams

## Tradeoffs

- adds governance structure to post-release support
- some stabilization indicators may be hard to interpret quickly
- teams may debate thresholds if they are not agreed up front

## Common Risks

- hypercare ending before true stabilization
- hypercare dragging on because criteria were vague
- exit evidence captured inconsistently across teams
