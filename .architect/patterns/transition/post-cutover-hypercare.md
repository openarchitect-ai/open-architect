# Post-Cutover Hypercare

## Purpose

Provide an intensified support and monitoring period immediately after cutover
so stabilization issues are detected quickly and resolved with clear ownership.

## When To Use

- a transition or go-live has meaningful business or operational risk
- early-life support needs to be stronger than normal steady-state support
- rapid learning and issue resolution are important after change activation

## When Not To Use

- the cutover is trivial and needs no special post-event support window
- no additional support posture can actually be staffed or managed

## Assumptions

- a defined hypercare window can be agreed
- ownership and escalation paths are clear
- monitoring, communication, and issue triage can be intensified temporarily

## Key Components

- hypercare duration and exit criteria
- dedicated support and triage path
- enhanced monitoring and communication cadence
- transition from hypercare to steady-state ownership

## Benefits

- improves early stabilization after go-live
- reduces uncertainty during the most fragile period
- gives teams a clear model for intensified support

## Tradeoffs

- requires extra staffing or operational attention
- can blur normal support ownership if exit criteria are unclear
- may become extended by default if readiness is weak

## Common Risks

- no clear end to hypercare
- issues found but not folded into longer-term remediation
- steady-state teams not prepared to take over
