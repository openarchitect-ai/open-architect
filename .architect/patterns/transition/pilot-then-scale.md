# Pilot Then Scale

## Purpose

Introduce a solution or change to a limited scope first, learn from real use,
and only then expand to broader rollout with better evidence and lower risk.

## When To Use

- uncertainty is high and controlled learning is valuable
- broad rollout risk would be hard to justify immediately
- early adopters or contained segments can provide meaningful feedback

## When Not To Use

- a pilot would add delay without reducing any real uncertainty
- the solution has no practical limited-scope entry point

## Assumptions

- pilot scope and success criteria can be defined
- the pilot can produce evidence relevant to later scale decisions
- expansion beyond the pilot is a real option, not just hope

## Key Components

- pilot scope and population
- success and exit criteria
- learning and feedback loop
- scale-out decision and follow-on rollout path

## Benefits

- reduces uncertainty before broad rollout
- creates evidence for design or governance refinement
- improves confidence in scaling decisions

## Tradeoffs

- adds an extra stage to delivery
- can create temporary complexity or dual processes
- risks stalling if success criteria are unclear

## Common Risks

- pilots with no explicit scale decision point
- lessons learned not being folded into wider rollout
- pilot conditions unrepresentative of real-scale use
