# Capability-Based Planning

## Purpose

Plan change around business capabilities so initiatives, priorities, and
solution work are anchored to stable business needs rather than only to
organizational structures or short-lived projects.

## When To Use

- multiple projects or solution options compete for the same business outcomes
- the architecture needs a stable business anchor for prioritization
- capability gaps should guide roadmap and transition choices

## When Not To Use

- the problem is too small or local to justify a capability lens
- capabilities would be invented superficially with no real planning use

## Assumptions

- meaningful capabilities can be identified and agreed
- business owners can validate capability priorities
- capability links can influence roadmap or scope decisions

## Key Components

- capability map or focused capability set
- capability ownership and priority
- linkage to objectives, gaps, and initiatives
- planning decisions based on capability impact

## Benefits

- improves strategic coherence across projects
- provides a stable business anchor for architecture
- supports clearer prioritization of change

## Tradeoffs

- adds business framing effort
- can become abstract if not tied to real decisions
- requires consistency in capability definitions

## Common Risks

- treating capabilities as labels with no planning consequence
- duplicating organizational charts as capability models
- weak linkage from capability priorities to actual architecture choices
