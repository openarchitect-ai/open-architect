# Business Capability Heatmap

## Purpose

Assess business capabilities visually and comparatively so architecture work can
focus attention on areas with the greatest change need, risk exposure, or
investment priority.

## When To Use

- architecture planning needs a quick view of capability health or strategic fit
- investment choices need to be compared across multiple capabilities
- change programs need to show where weakness, duplication, or strategic
  importance is concentrated

## When Not To Use

- capability definitions are too immature for comparative scoring
- the heatmap would be used as a substitute for deeper analysis where detail is
  required

## Assumptions

- capabilities can be defined consistently
- scoring dimensions are agreed and explained clearly
- the heatmap will support prioritization, not act as the only decision input

## Key Components

- capability map
- scoring dimensions such as criticality, maturity, pain, or investment need
- visual rating scale
- interpretation guidance and decision follow-through

## Benefits

- gives a fast view of where attention is needed most
- helps align business and architecture priorities
- supports investment and transition discussions with a shared visual

## Tradeoffs

- scoring can oversimplify complex capability realities
- different stakeholders may interpret the same rating differently
- the heatmap needs periodic refresh to stay useful

## Common Risks

- subjective scoring without clear criteria
- confusing importance with maturity
- using the heatmap without linking it to objectives or change actions
