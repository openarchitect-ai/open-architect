# Capability Funding Alignment

## Purpose

Align funding decisions to business capabilities so investment follows enduring
business need rather than only temporary project structures or organizational
boundaries.

## When To Use

- multiple initiatives contribute to the same core capabilities
- funding conversations are fragmented across projects with overlapping outcomes
- architecture planning needs clearer linkage between strategy and investment

## When Not To Use

- the scope is too small or short-lived for capability-oriented funding to add
  value
- capability definitions are too immature to support meaningful alignment

## Assumptions

- capabilities can be defined and prioritized clearly
- funding owners can understand capability-level outcomes
- investment decisions will influence roadmap and architecture choices

## Key Components

- capability model
- funding ownership and decision model
- mapping from initiatives to capabilities
- prioritization and review cycle

## Benefits

- improves visibility of where investment is really going
- reduces duplicate or conflicting funding across related change efforts
- supports longer-term capability development over one-off project thinking

## Tradeoffs

- requires stronger capability definitions and governance
- may complicate existing budget structures
- demands coordination across business and delivery stakeholders

## Common Risks

- capabilities defined too broadly to guide investment well
- funding still driven by local politics despite the model
- weak traceability from spending to capability outcomes
