# Product Operating Model Alignment

## Purpose

Align product structure, ownership, and operating expectations with the
underlying architecture so team boundaries, accountability, and delivery flow
support the intended solution model.

## When To Use

- product teams and architecture boundaries do not line up clearly
- ownership confusion is causing friction across products and shared services
- organizational design materially affects architecture outcomes

## When Not To Use

- the delivery context is too small for operating-model alignment to matter
- the issue is purely technical and not influenced by ownership or team shape

## Assumptions

- product boundaries and responsibilities can be described clearly
- operating-model changes can influence delivery behavior
- the architecture needs to reflect how teams will actually work

## Key Components

- product and platform boundary definition
- ownership and accountability model
- team interaction and escalation model
- architectural implications for service, data, and platform boundaries

## Benefits

- reduces mismatch between architecture and delivery reality
- improves accountability for shared and product-specific concerns
- supports more sustainable product and platform evolution

## Tradeoffs

- can surface organizational tensions that are harder than technical issues
- alignment work may take longer than expected
- too much optimization for current structure can reduce future flexibility

## Common Risks

- architecture shaped around organization politics rather than outcomes
- unclear product boundaries creating duplication and conflict
- team changes invalidating assumptions without architecture being revisited
