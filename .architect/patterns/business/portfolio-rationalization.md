# Portfolio Rationalization

## Purpose

Assess overlapping applications, platforms, or solution options systematically
so the portfolio can be simplified, modernized, consolidated, or retired with
clear business and architecture rationale.

## When To Use

- multiple systems serve similar needs
- cost, complexity, or inconsistency has grown across the estate
- strategy requires deciding what to invest in, tolerate, migrate, or retire

## When Not To Use

- the scope is too small for portfolio-level analysis to add value
- there is only one viable system choice and no meaningful rationalization
  decision remains

## Assumptions

- comparable systems or services can be identified
- evaluation criteria can balance business value, risk, cost, and fit
- the outcome will influence roadmap or investment decisions

## Key Components

- in-scope portfolio set
- evaluation criteria and evidence
- target disposition such as invest, tolerate, migrate, or retire
- transition implications for chosen dispositions

## Benefits

- reduces unmanaged duplication
- supports clearer investment prioritization
- improves strategic coherence of the application and technology estate

## Tradeoffs

- requires evidence gathering and stakeholder alignment
- can expose politically sensitive ownership questions
- may oversimplify if criteria are too coarse

## Common Risks

- decisions made on cost alone without business context
- rationalization outcomes not flowing into real roadmaps
- hidden integration or data dependencies undermining retirement plans
