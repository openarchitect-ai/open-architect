# Business Capability Roadmapping

## Purpose

Plan the evolution of business capabilities over time so target-state ambition,
sequencing, and investment choices are visible beyond individual projects.

## When To Use

- capability improvement spans multiple initiatives or planning cycles
- architecture needs to show how business capability change unfolds over time
- business and technology stakeholders need a shared long-term planning view

## When Not To Use

- the scope is too small or immediate for roadmap thinking to add value
- capabilities are not yet defined clearly enough to support staged planning

## Assumptions

- capabilities can be assessed for current and target state
- sequencing decisions will influence funding or delivery priorities
- roadmap milestones can be maintained as context changes

## Key Components

- baseline and target capability position
- transition milestones
- linked initiatives and dependencies
- review and reprioritization cadence

## Benefits

- improves long-term planning visibility
- helps align initiatives to enduring business outcomes
- supports more coherent sequencing of change

## Tradeoffs

- requires maintenance as priorities evolve
- can oversimplify capability change if treated as purely linear
- needs stronger governance than one-off project roadmaps

## Common Risks

- roadmap divorced from real delivery constraints
- capability targets described too vaguely to guide decisions
- initiatives progressing without updating the roadmap
