# Waiver Expiry And Remediation

## Purpose

Ensure exceptions, waivers, or temporary non-conformances do not remain open
indefinitely by defining expiry, review, and remediation expectations up front.

## When To Use

- a standard, principle, or control cannot be met immediately
- a temporary exception needs a clear end state
- governance requires follow-through rather than permanent waiver drift

## When Not To Use

- the situation should be handled as a durable change to the standard itself
- no meaningful remediation path exists and pretending otherwise adds noise

## Assumptions

- waiver ownership can be assigned
- expiry or review timing can be stated
- remediation actions can be described and tracked

## Key Components

- waiver scope and rationale
- expiry or review trigger
- remediation path and owner
- escalation path if remediation does not occur

## Benefits

- prevents temporary exceptions from becoming permanent by neglect
- improves governance accountability
- makes remediation work visible early

## Tradeoffs

- adds lifecycle management overhead
- may require uncomfortable escalation when remediation slips
- depends on disciplined follow-up

## Common Risks

- expiry dates set with no intention to enforce them
- remediation work not tied to real delivery planning
- waivers renewed repeatedly without strategic review
