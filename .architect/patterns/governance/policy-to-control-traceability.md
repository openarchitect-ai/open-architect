# Policy To Control Traceability

## Purpose

Trace high-level policies through to concrete control implementations so
architecture, compliance, and delivery teams can see how intent becomes
enforced behavior.

## When To Use

- policy statements need clearer operational interpretation
- control design spans multiple platforms, services, or teams
- review and assurance depend on showing how policy intent is realized

## When Not To Use

- the scope is too small for formal traceability to add value
- policy itself is still too immature to map meaningfully to controls

## Assumptions

- policy statements can be decomposed into control objectives
- controls can be identified and linked to responsible owners
- traceability will inform review, remediation, or assurance activity

## Key Components

- policy statements
- mapped control objectives
- implemented controls and owners
- evidence and review links

## Benefits

- improves transparency from governance intent to real implementation
- supports stronger audit and compliance readiness
- helps reveal gaps between policy language and technical control coverage

## Tradeoffs

- requires ongoing maintenance as policy and platforms change
- can become documentation-heavy if not scoped carefully
- demands coordination between policy, risk, and engineering teams

## Common Risks

- mappings created once and left stale
- assumed coverage without validating actual control behavior
- traceability too high-level to support real decisions
