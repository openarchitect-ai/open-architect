# Standard Lifecycle Management

## Purpose

Manage architecture standards through creation, review, revision, supersession,
and retirement so they remain relevant and governable over time.

## When To Use

- technology or architecture standards guide multiple projects
- tolerated, approved, or prohibited positions change over time
- governance needs a clear process for evolving standards deliberately

## When Not To Use

- the guidance is too local or short-lived to justify lifecycle management
- the rule is really a one-off project decision rather than a standard

## Assumptions

- standard ownership can be assigned
- review triggers or review cadence can be defined
- projects can trace conformance or exception handling against the standard

## Key Components

- standard definition and scope
- status and lifecycle transitions
- review and update process
- supersession, retirement, or migration guidance

## Benefits

- keeps standards current and actionable
- reduces accumulation of obsolete governance
- improves traceability from standards to conformance outcomes

## Tradeoffs

- adds governance overhead
- requires sustained ownership discipline
- may trigger broader change-management work when standards shift

## Common Risks

- outdated standards remaining active by default
- projects following superseded guidance unknowingly
- no explicit migration path when standard posture changes
