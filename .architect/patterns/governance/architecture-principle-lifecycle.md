# Architecture Principle Lifecycle

## Purpose

Manage architecture principles as living governance assets that can be created,
reviewed, revised, superseded, and retired as the organization and technology
context change.

## When To Use

- principles guide multiple projects or standards over time
- old principles risk becoming outdated or contradictory
- governance needs a clear way to maintain principle relevance

## When Not To Use

- the rule is project-specific and better captured as a decision
- lifecycle management would add ceremony without real governance value

## Assumptions

- principle ownership can be assigned
- review triggers or review cadence can be stated
- related standards and decisions can be linked back to principles

## Key Components

- principle definition and rationale
- approval and ownership
- review, supersession, and retirement path
- linkages to standards, decisions, and exceptions

## Benefits

- keeps principles relevant and governable
- reduces accumulation of contradictory guidance
- improves traceability from enduring rules to project choices

## Tradeoffs

- requires ongoing governance effort
- may expose disagreement about whether a rule is really a principle
- can drift if review discipline is weak

## Common Risks

- obsolete principles staying active by default
- decisions and standards no longer aligned to current principles
- no explicit ownership for updating or retiring principles
