# Decision Authority Matrix

## Purpose

Define who can decide what across architecture domains so escalation, approval,
and accountability stay clear as work spans teams and governance levels.

## When To Use

- architecture decisions cross multiple roles or authority levels
- delays happen because decision rights are unclear
- governance needs explicit boundaries between local and escalated choices

## When Not To Use

- the delivery context is simple enough that informal decision ownership is
  sufficient
- the matrix would be created but not actually used by teams

## Assumptions

- decision categories can be grouped meaningfully
- named roles or forums can hold authority
- escalation paths will follow the matrix in practice

## Key Components

- decision categories
- authority owner for each category
- escalation thresholds
- communication and exception handling rules

## Benefits

- reduces ambiguity and delay in decision-making
- improves consistency of governance behavior
- supports better separation of local autonomy and central control

## Tradeoffs

- requires upkeep as roles and governance structures change
- too much detail can make the matrix hard to use
- rigid authority boundaries can slow practical collaboration

## Common Risks

- matrix documented but ignored under pressure
- decision categories too vague to guide action
- overlapping authority causing conflict instead of clarity
