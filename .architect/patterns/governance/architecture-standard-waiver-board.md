# Architecture Standard Waiver Board

## Purpose

Provide a formal governance forum for reviewing, approving, rejecting, and
tracking waivers against architecture standards so exceptions remain visible and
controlled.

## When To Use

- multiple teams request exceptions to architecture standards
- waiver decisions need consistent authority and traceability
- standards compliance has meaningful risk, cost, or interoperability impact

## When Not To Use

- a lightweight local exception process is sufficient for the context
- the issue can be resolved by normal interpretation of the standard without a
  waiver

## Assumptions

- architecture standards and waiver criteria exist
- the board has clear authority and decision rights
- waiver outcomes will be reflected in downstream plans and reviews

## Key Components

- waiver submission and evidence pack
- review forum and decision criteria
- approval duration or expiry rules
- remediation, follow-up, and reporting

## Benefits

- improves consistency of waiver decisions
- keeps exceptions visible to governance stakeholders
- supports stronger long-term standard integrity

## Tradeoffs

- adds process overhead
- can slow delivery if the board is overloaded
- requires disciplined follow-through after approval

## Common Risks

- waivers approved without strong evidence
- board becoming a rubber-stamp mechanism
- expired waivers not being revisited or remediated
